3 November 2026Journal entryNot held; cost limit reachedref RC26-0375 (including RC26-0353, RC26-0368, RC26-0371)

Two replies from the Commission's public enquiries team (13:13 and 13:14 UTC) to the requester's emails of 29 October 2026, which supplied further historical names for the site and asked about three title records of 1926 and 1938.

Assessment (our reading): Reply on time and reasoned. The Commission explains why new names would not change its result (a location-based search) and cites the statutory cost limit, a recognised response after several related requests. It does not say which names its earlier searches used, does not show how the cost limit was reached, and does not state whether this is a formal refusal with review rights. Those three points are the open questions.

What they did

  • Stated that every record it holds for the site is listed in the NMRW archive catalogue for Coflein site 20182.
  • Stated that its earlier searches included geographical searches of the site and its records as well as term-based searches, and that it is confident the additional search terms would have no bearing on the results.
  • Stated that it does not hold the three title records named (a 1926 conveyance and plan, 1926 Mountjoy Ltd company records, a 1938 conveyance and plan), has no record of having held them, and that they are not record types generally held in the National Monuments Record.
  • Stated that staff time on searches for this matter and site has passed its FOI cost limit of £450 and the half-hour limit for general enquiries, and that any further search would be a chargeable complex enquiry (up to three hours).
  • Pointed to its online catalogue (Coflein) for the requester's own further searches.

What they did not do

  • Did not re-run the term-based search with the names supplied on 29 October 2026 (for example Llandough Home Farm, the 1881 census form, and the Cydfin / Cedfin forms).
  • Did not list which names and terms the earlier term-based searches used. Its 6 October reply described them as all relevant site names and related terms as the requester had listed them, without naming them.
  • Did not give a breakdown of the time or cost estimate behind the £450 figure, or say which requests were counted together.
  • Did not state whether the reply is a refusal under section 12 of the Freedom of Information Act 2000, or name the internal review and Information Commissioner routes.

Fair note

  • The £450 is not a quoted fee. It is the appropriate limit for public authorities outside central government under the Freedom of Information and Data Protection (Appropriate Limit and Fees) Regulations 2004 (18 hours at £25 an hour). Regulation 5 lets an authority add together the cost of similar requests from the same person made within 60 working days. With four related requests in September 2026 and several follow-ups, citing the limit is a recognised response.
  • A geographical search by site location (NPRN 20182, NGR ST16707335) does not depend on what the site was called, so the reason given for not re-running name searches holds for records indexed to the site. It carries less weight for records indexed only by an estate or company name without a location, although the Commission also states that conveyances and company records are not record types it generally holds.
  • Time taken: the new names were sent on 29 October 2026 and the reply came three working days later. Earlier substantive replies (29 September, 6, 13 and 26 October 2026) came within the 20 working days set by section 10. On the record so far the Commission has given the matter steady attention.
  • Section 16 expects advice and assistance. Pointing to the public catalogue is one form of it. Guidance from the Information Commissioner also expects an authority relying on the cost limit to suggest how a request could be narrowed to fit within it.

Open questions

  • Which site names and terms did the term-based searches of September and October 2026 use?
  • How was the cost estimate made (hours, activities, and which requests were counted together)?
  • Is the reply of 3 November 2026 a section 12 refusal notice, with a right to internal review, or a statement about how future enquiries will be handled?
  • Would a single narrowed search, for example the 1881 form Llandough Home Farm only, fit within what remains of the limit?
