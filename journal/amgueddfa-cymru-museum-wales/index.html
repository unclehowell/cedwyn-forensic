4 November 2026Journal entryNot held (re-search; final)ref Museum Wales FOI 2026-020 (Cardiff Castle furniture)

Email of 4 November 2026, 15:16 UTC, from the Museum Wales FOI Team on FOI 2026-020, answering the requester's email of 29 October 2026, which asked for the searches on the original request of 2 July 2026 to be rerun or extended with additional name forms for the site, and for the terms, indexes, catalogues and series checked to be identified. The request covers recorded information about furniture, furnishings, antiques, fixtures, heirlooms and other movable property originating from the site. The internal review of FOI 2026-020 was completed on 8 September 2026.

Assessment (our reading): A prompt and courteous final answer: nothing held after a further check. The explanation of indexing is reasonable. What it lacks is the description of searches that the requester asked for, which is the one point an ICO complaint would test.

What they did

  • Replied four working days after the 29 October email.
  • Collections staff reviewed the additional terms and explained that records for the locality would be indexed under "Llandough", and that the additional terms are variants of Llandough or of the farm name rather than materially different identifiers.
  • Ran additional checks against relevant place-name variants; no further records were identified and the outcome of the original searches is unchanged.
  • Stated that no records about furniture, furnishings, antiques, fixtures, heirlooms or other movable property originating from Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr have been identified, and that it has no further information to provide on FOI 2026-020.

What they did not do

  • Did not list the collections systems, indexes or record series searched, which the 29 October email asked for.
  • Did not say which place-name variants were checked.
  • Did not state whether object or acquisition files for Cardiff Castle items were checked by provenance as well as by place index.

Fair note

  • After an internal review the authority's process is complete, and the next route is a complaint to the Information Commissioner under s.50. Treating the new terms as a re-check within days, rather than as a new request taking 20 working days, is helpful.
  • Indexing by the standard parish name is normal museum practice, so a search under "Llandough" would catch most of the variant forms.
  • ICO guidance on the adequacy of searches expects an authority to be able to explain what it searched; a short description of the systems checked would complete the answer.

Open questions

  • Which collections systems, indexes and series were searched?
  • Were Cardiff Castle object and acquisition files checked for provenance from Llandough-juxta-Penarth?
