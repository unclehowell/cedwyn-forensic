Finding

Our reading: The 1987 Court of Appeal judgment establishes that counsel for Mr W. Buckler were instructed by Robbins Olivey & Blake Lapthorn, acting as agents for Messrs Loosemores of Cardiff. The judgment does not identify Mark Loosemore as a solicitor on the case. His published career history says that he qualified in 1996 and was Senior Partner of Loosemores Solicitors from 2016 to 2023. His statement that he joined Loosemores in 2016 and has no personal background knowledge of the 1987 case is therefore consistent with that public chronology.

The material issue is not an established contradiction in Mark's career account. It is the evidential basis for the firm's present file-custody position. Mark said he believed Loosemores may have acted for Mr Buckler, did not believe relevant client-file records or property documents were currently held, and believed an 1980s file would have been destroyed several years earlier. Those statements were expressly framed as beliefs. No search schedule, archive inventory, transfer list, disposal date or destruction record was supplied.

The available material does not establish that Mark, Loosemores or Hugh James acted dishonestly, destroyed a file improperly, or concealed evidence. Equally, the reply is not a verified record that the file was searched for and destroyed. The correct present classification is historic representation established; modern custody and disposal unverified; no misconduct established.

This is a correspondence-led preliminary review of firm identity, the 1987 representation and the later records-custody response. It is not a finding about an individual's honesty, competence or conduct in an unrelated matter, and it is not legal advice.

The 1987 judgment establishes a firm-level role, not Mark's personal involvement

The Court of Appeal's front sheet records that J. B. W. McDonnell Q.C. and M. Jarman appeared for the appellant, instructed by Robbins Olivey & Blake Lapthorn, acting as agents for Messrs Loosemores of Cardiff. This is direct evidence that the firm name Loosemores of Cardiff was part of the appellant's representation on the appeal. It does not identify the individual solicitor at Loosemores who held the retainer, handled the file or gave instructions, and it does not by itself establish who acted at every earlier stage of the proceedings.

The judgment can be read in the project's copy of BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2 (EV-0080). The judgment is the primary source for the 1987 appearance. It should take priority over uncertainty in later recollection.

Mark Loosemore's published career chronology is internally consistent

Hugh James's current professional profile identifies Mark Loosemore as a partner in its corporate and commercial team. Its career history records legal qualification in 1996 and Senior Partner at Loosemores Solicitors from 2016 to 2023. The same profile says that he joined Hugh James in 2023 after Loosemores was acquired.

Those dates do not conflict with his statement that he joined Loosemores in 2016. They explain why he would not have personal working knowledge of a 1987 case. He was not yet qualified as a solicitor when the Court of Appeal heard the appeal. The 1987 judgment does not name him. No conclusion should be drawn that he personally represented the family, prepared the 1987 case, or held the original file.

The published profile gives his current practice focus as corporate/commercial work, including sport, media and entertainment, and hospitality/leisure. This page does not assess his competence in those areas. It records his role only insofar as it explains the 2016–2023 firm-management period and the 2023 transition.

The 2023 acquisition is established; transfer of the 1987 archive is not

Hugh James announced on 3 July 2023 that it had acquired Loosemores Solicitors and that Mark Loosemore and 27 employees joined Hugh James. The Solicitors Regulation Authority's register records the Cardiff firm LOOSEMORES, SRA number 61408, as closed on 30 June 2023. Those records support the broad practice-level connection between the firm that ceased operating under that SRA record and Hugh James's 2023 acquisition announcement.

Neither source states whether the 1986–1987 Buckler litigation file, old file indexes, off-site storage records, deeds or destruction schedules were transferred to Hugh James, retained by a separate archive provider, or destroyed before the acquisition. A business acquisition and an SRA closure entry do not, on their own, prove the custody history of a particular client file.

A separate company-name history record concerning John Loosemore & Co. Services / Loosemores Services is retained as a lead at EV-0267. It is a separate corporate-name record. It does not establish that the service company was the regulated legal practice, that it held the Buckler client file, or that it transferred any archive to Hugh James. The page title therefore does not assert a proven legal succession from John Loosemore & Co.

The reply is a qualified belief, not a certified archive search result

In November 2026, the firm contact first said he was looking into the historic file question. In a later reply, he said he believed Loosemores might have acted for Mr W. Buckler in the late 1980s; he did not believe relevant client-file records or property documents were currently held; and he believed any 1980s file would have been destroyed several years ago. On 19 November, he said that he had no background knowledge of the father's case because he had only joined Loosemores in 2016, and declined to assist with the separate 1971 Ordnance Survey question.

The statement that Loosemores might have acted is cautious, but the Court of Appeal judgment independently establishes that Messrs Loosemores of Cardiff acted as agents' instructing firm for the appellant's counsel. The remaining questions concern the identity of the particular practice entity and whether its later archive can be traced. The response did not say which systems, indexes, storage locations, predecessor names or case references had been searched; who conducted the search; or whether the asserted destruction was confirmed by a register or other contemporaneous record.

The phrase “destroyed several years ago” is not a date. It does not identify who authorised disposal, under which policy, which file series was covered, whether the file was paper or electronic, or whether duplicates, counsel's papers, court papers, deeds or other records survived elsewhere. The absence of a supplied disposal record does not prove that destruction was unlawful or that records still exist.

Routine destruction of an old closed file is possible; the specific disposal remains unverified

The SRA's guidance published in 2013 and updated in February 2022 says closed files should not be kept indefinitely and that firms should apply an appropriate and rigorous destruction policy while preserving confidentiality. It describes options that include returning files, arranging storage with another firm, destroying old files, or scanning documents. It says current clients must be informed when a practice closes; notification of former clients may be good practice where they may be affected, particularly where the firm holds documents such as wills or title deeds.

The Law Society's 2023 practice-advice answer says there is no single specific retention period for every closed client file; firms should set a period having regard to relevant limitation periods, the file's subject matter, client-care terms and other requirements. This current professional guidance is context for assessing the plausibility of old-file destruction; it is not a retrospective determination of what policy applied when the Buckler file may have been disposed of.

A 1980s litigation file could therefore have been destroyed through a lawful, properly managed retention process. The existence of a long-closed file does not create a duty to retain it forever. Conversely, if the file was destroyed, the precise date and policy matter: the present replies do not establish either. The SRA register's statement that no regulatory decisions are published for LOOSEMORES is not a certificate of unblemished conduct; the SRA explains that its publication policy does not publish every decision.

Evidence needed to close the custody question

  • Identify the legal practice name and entity that traded as Messrs Loosemores of Cardiff in 1986–1988, and establish whether it is the same practice later regulated under SRA number 61408.
  • Identify the 1986–1987 matter reference, file index entry, former client ledger entry or archive locator for Mr W. Buckler / BP Properties Ltd v Buckler.
  • Establish what archive systems, off-site storage providers, legacy indexes and predecessor names were checked in 2026, including who conducted the searches and when.
  • Locate any retention schedule, file-closure notation, destruction register, disposal certificate or archive-provider instruction identifying the file and its disposal date.
  • Establish what historic client-file indexes and storage records were included in the 2023 acquisition or transition, and whether any pre-acquisition archive remained with Loosemores, a third-party storage company or another custodian.
  • Check separately whether original deeds, title papers, counsel's papers, court papers or a duplicate file were held by another party. The Court of Appeal's judgment and the court's own record are separate sources and do not depend on the solicitor's file surviving.

The most useful next step is a single records-management enquiry to the firm's appropriate archive or risk-management custodian, requesting a factual statement of the search scope and any disposal/transfer record. Mark has said he has no personal background knowledge and does not think he can assist further; the remaining question is institutional records custody, not his personal recollection.

Limits and present conclusion

No evidence reviewed establishes dishonesty, fraud, improper destruction, concealment, breach of professional duty or personal involvement by Mark Loosemore in the 1987 proceedings. His career dates are consistent. The 1987 judgment establishes that Loosemores of Cardiff acted for the appellant at the appeal, but the individual solicitor and legal entity behind that name are not yet fully identified. The 2023 acquisition is documented, but no material presently establishes the fate of the old Buckler file.

The assessment should change only if a primary record contradicts the chronology or establishes an identifiable failure in file transfer, retention, disposal or disclosure. The current position is an unresolved archival question, not an adverse finding.
