Full record: ITV Wales / HTV and Tŷ Mawr, 1968–2026

Added 5 November 2026 · 27 dated entries · read with the carried-over assessment below

Objective read: Records now show HTV Wales reported the dispute over Tŷ Mawr (Great House), the manorial house / former court house, at least twice in 1988 (12 May and 30 November, both National Library of Wales catalogue records naming the place and the occupiers), and broadcast two Report Wales items in 1974 (11 April and 1 November) about protests at “a cottage at Llandough”, whose identification with Tŷ Mawr is not checked. This replaces the earlier line (“whether HTV Wales filmed or reported it is not known”) for 1974 with catalogue evidence, subject to viewing. All four items are “Not viewed” by the cataloguer and sit on cassettes or reels with unrelated material. ITV is a private company, not a public authority under FOIA; its desks answered with standard automated routing, and its archivist replied in person and arranged a call, which is ordinary courtesy rather than a search undertaking. Since 2012 the physical HTV holdings are at the National Library of Wales, a public authority, so the Library is the statutory route to its own holdings (FOI2026/17 is open), while reuse still needs ITV’s permission. No record reviewed shows ITV withholding, editing or destroying material; the 1988 edit history cannot be tested until the original transmissions or rushes are viewed.

What the records show

  • Four HTV Wales items in the Library’s ITV collection: 11 April 1974 (CAN 2775), 1 November 1974 (CAN 2904), 12 May 1988 (MMS 99226117302419), 30 November 1988 (MMS 99213668002419 / Watch and Listen 507738).
  • Named HTV staff on those items: Max Perkins (April 1974), David Bellin (November 1974), Miles Fletcher (1988); presenter on the GHF-E113 copy: Nicola Heywood-Thomas.
  • 2025–2026 contact: 15 messages in the correspondence register under ITV, plus the 14 September 2025 archive-account notice and the 14 May 2026 ITN email; three personal replies from ITV staff (13, 14 and 16 October 2026) and one phone call (14 October 2026); every other ITV reply was automated.

What is not checked

  • The content of every item listed (none has been viewed).
  • Whether the 1974 “cottage at Llandough” is Tŷ Mawr.
  • Any HTV coverage of 15 April 1974, 29 April 1988 and 5–6 December 1988.
  • Which court step the 12 May 1988 and 30 November 1988 headlines describe, against the court record.

14 October 2026 — ITV phone call: open questions, risks and potential remedy

Evidence basis: the family’s account of his phone conversation with Owen at ITV. The account is not an independently verified transcript. Statements below are questions for investigation, not findings of wrongdoing.

The unresolved editorial questions

  • Family context and possible omission: the family states that the clip he has viewed does not mention that his mother was pregnant or that three infant children were part of the family context. Was that information included in the original broadcast? If not, was its absence an editorial choice, an instruction, a time/format limitation, or simply a difference between the surviving clip and the transmitted report?
  • Did the clip air in this form? The exact programme, transmission date, running order, original broadcast copy and length have not been identified. The extant viewing copy cannot by itself establish what viewers saw in 1988.
  • Abrupt cut and later editing: After the voice-over describes the land as “prime development land overlooking Cardiff” and the clip shows a person the family has interpreted as a BP bailiff with a paramilitary appearance, the copy cuts abruptly. Was this the original broadcast edit, a later re-edit, a rights/licensing extract, a technical cut or a later editorial decision? Was any material removed before the clip was published online in 2025? No censorship finding is made.
  • Instructions, confidentiality or NDA: Is there documentary evidence of any instruction to omit the family context, or any confidentiality agreement that covered the story? No NDA has been identified. If an agreement existed, who was a party, when was it made, what material and subject matter did it cover, and was it story-specific or a standard/general agreement? It would be unsafe to assume that Nicola Heywood-Thomas was bound by any such agreement.
  • Presenter’s personal connection: the family’s personal recollection is that Nicola Heywood-Thomas was the mother of his best friend and felt like a second mother to him in approximately 2001–2003. He does not recall her mentioning the Great House Farm coverage or footage. That recollection explains why the question matters to him, but neither silence nor personal connection proves that she knew of the 1988 case, had a duty to disclose anything, or was subject to restrictions.
